Contents

Glossary

HOLD / EOT
The two labels. HOLD: the caller paused but has not finished. EOT (end of turn): the caller has finished and expects an answer.
False cutoff
Deciding EOT during a HOLD pause; the caller is interrupted. Counted per mid-sentence pause between 0.2 and 5 s.
Endpointing delay
Time from the caller's last word to the agent's decision to speak, averaged over real turn ends. Excludes model compute.
Interruption budget
The share of mid-sentence pauses a policy is allowed to cut. Detectors are compared at the same budget, usually 5%.
Policy
Score threshold, action delay and timeout that turn a score into a decision. Chosen on the benchmark, so every comparison is conditional on it.
EoT Bench
LiveKit's public benchmark: 400 real English turns, 1,250 pauses, human annotations. Harness commit 6594d8b3, data revision ca9d98a9.
Krisp test set
An independent, test-only set of 2,730 clips from 30 speakers. Its licence forbids training. AUC = area under the ROC curve at the 0.2 s score point.
Held-out accents
Real-call examples from three accents never used in training, our third external check.
Smart Turn v3.2
Pipecat's open-weights end-of-turn model (Whisper-Tiny encoder). Our starting weights and the public reference.
LiveKit Turn Detector v1
Closed model that leads the public benchmark at 543 ms. Its licence forbids using its outputs for training.
Whisper-Tiny / Whisper-base
OpenAI speech encoders with approximately 8M and 20M encoder parameters, used as the audio backbone.
Cohere Transcribe
A 1.9B-parameter bidirectional speech encoder (Apache-2.0) used in the research branch. Adapted with LoRA: one fixed teacher for the controlled study, then three continued teachers for expanded-data training.
LoRA
Low-rank adapters: small trainable matrices added to a frozen model. Rank 16 here, 28.2M trainable parameters.
Seed
The random initialisation of a training run. Three seeds per recipe; replication measures sensitivity to training randomness.
Paired bootstrap
Resampling the same turns for two models to get an interval on their difference. Descriptive here, because policies were chosen on the benchmark.
Knowledge distillation
A frozen, EoT-adapted Cohere teacher provides probability targets for Whisper-base. Temperature-scaled Bernoulli KL (T=2); identical realized audio for both. A 2×2 experiment separates this from pause-texture augmentation.
Gate
A pass/fail rule declared before running an experiment. A model is promoted only if every gate passes.
Prefix mining
Cutting a clip at each pause and labelling the audio before the cut by what happened after it.
Behavioural oracle
Labelling a pause in a two-channel call by what the human agent did next (AppTek call-centre corpus, 873 role-play calls).
Silero VAD
Open-source voice-activity detector, used as the second opinion in the pause detector.
ONNX, FP32, INT8
ONNX is a portable graph format. FP32 uses 32-bit floating point; INT8 uses 8-bit integers for supported operations. The original ONNX QAT artifact failed; the native CPU artifact passed its own 97-probe check.
p95, concurrency
The latency below which 95% of requests complete; the number of requests in flight at once.
8-bit training and native CPU runtime
Quantization-aware training exposes the model to quantization during training. The saved TorchScript CPU runtime executes 36 learned projections in INT8, retaining floating-point operations elsewhere.
AUC / BCE
AUC measures how well scores rank positive versus negative examples across thresholds. Binary cross-entropy (BCE) scores probability predictions. Neither directly measures waiting at a chosen cutoff budget.
Prediction ensemble / weight soup
An ensemble combines outputs of multiple models and pays for multiple forwards. A weight soup averages compatible checkpoint weights into one model. Both require their own quality evaluation.
Pause augmentation (pause texture)
Replace some pause tails with digital silence or low-level pink noise, leaving speech and the end-of-turn label unchanged. This reduces the opportunity to use recording artifacts as a shortcut. Its added benefit is supported on EoT Bench, but unresolved on Krisp.
More pause examples (dense targets)
Cut training audio at additional points on a 100 ms grid inside eligible pauses. Each cut contains only audio available up to that instant. Three Cohere teachers supply probability targets to Whisper; no new manual labels are created. The final experiment combines this with more data and continued teacher training.
Continued teacher training
Further fine-tune the same Cohere architecture on the expanded data with pause augmentation. The earlier label “stronger teacher” referred to improved task performance, not a larger model.
EoT classification head
A small learned classifier converts the audio encoder’s representation into a probability of turn completion. Whisper Tiny, Whisper-base and Cohere all have one. Head-only training freezes the encoder; it is one Cohere control experiment, not a missing component in Whisper.
Teacher / student
The teacher is the larger Cohere model adapted to recognize turn ends. The student is Whisper-base, trained to match its probabilities. Only the student is needed when serving a distilled model.
Selected CPU and GPU deployments
CPU: the best distilled Whisper-base checkpoint, 756 ms waiting, optimized ONNX. GPU: the best fine-tuned Cohere checkpoint, compiled with TensorRT. Each has its own Docker, artifact hash, runtime quality check and HTTP latency measurement. The 809 ms Whisper model is an earlier reference.

Evidence and model references

The main story has 12 slides. The appendix retains detailed experiments. These references identify models and the limits of reproduction.

  • Cohere · GPU: L041 seed222 fine-tuned checkpoint, compiled to TensorRT for RTX 3090. Quality and full HTTP measurements use the same engine. Exact precision, hashes and INT8 investigation are in the deployment receipt.
  • Whisper-base · distilled: L041 selected student, final checkpoint at learning rate 8e-5, 756.25 ms waiting. Selected by fixed validation AUC.
  • Whisper-base · CPU INT8: L041 final per-vector QAT, saved native CPU runtime, 774 ms waiting. Its full-precision control also waits 774 ms. Original ONNX QAT export remains rejected.

  • Earlier controlled studies: L031 compares frozen Cohere, upper-layer fine-tuning and all-layer LoRA. L040 compares hard labels, texture, distillation and their combination, with three runs per condition. Mean results are not ensembles.
  • Public comparison: 400 English turns, 705 eligible HOLD pauses and 400 EOTs; 11,191 aligned scored points. Public policies and ours are selected on this repeatedly inspected benchmark. Intervals condition on that selection; no blind-test or official leaderboard claim.
